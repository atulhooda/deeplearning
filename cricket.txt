CRICKET

Beneath the sun or floodlight's gleam,
Cricket lives like a waking dream.
A field of green, a willow's sound,
Where legends rise and tales are found.

From dusty lanes where barefoot boys
Chase every run with shrieks of joy,
To packed arenas roaring loud,
The game can gather every crowd.

A bat meets ball, the moment flies,
A fielder watches, sharp-eyed, wise.
The bowler runs with measured pace,
Then sends the ball towards its place.

Some balls are fast, some balls are slow,
Some swing away, some seam and go.
A batter waits with careful eyes,
Then drives the ball beneath the skies.

CRICKET AND ITS ORIGINS

Cricket grew through centuries of play,
Changing its shape along the way.
What started as a simple game
Would travel far and earn its name.

Villages gathered on open ground,
With wooden bats and cheers around.
The rules became more clearly known,
And cricket slowly found its own.

The pitch became a measured space,
With wickets standing face to face.
Three wooden stumps were set in line,
And players learned the laws divine.

The game spread across many lands,
Carried by people, ships, and hands.
Soon different nations formed their teams,
And cricket became a world of dreams.

THE CRICKET FIELD

The cricket field is wide and bright,
A circle changing shape in sight.
At its center lies the pitch,
Where every ball can change the switch.

The batting crease marks where players stand,
While fielders spread across the land.
A wicketkeeper waits behind,
Watching every movement, every sign.

At mid-off and cover players wait,
While square leg guards another gate.
Fine leg watches balls that glance,
And long-on waits for a mighty chance.

Every position has a role,
Every fielder guards the goal.
One careless step, one missed release,
Can turn a quiet over into peace or cease.

THE BAT

A cricket bat is shaped from wood,
Built to strike the ball as it should.
The willow carries marks of time,
Each stroke can turn into a rhyme.

A straight drive travels smooth and true,
A cover drive can split the two.
A pull can send the ball away,
While cuts can race through point all day.

The batter learns to watch the seam,
To judge the bounce and follow the dream.
The best technique looks calm and still,
Yet hides enormous strength and skill.

The bat is more than wood and grain,
It represents both hope and pain.
A single innings can create
A memory that can captivate.

THE BALL

The cricket ball is hard and round,
Its polished surface makes a sound.
The seam across its center line
Can make a delivery change its design.

A new ball shines beneath the light,
Its surface smooth, its leather tight.
As overs pass, one side may shine,
And bowlers use that change by design.

Fast bowlers search for movement late,
While spinners tempt the batter's fate.
The ball may dip, turn, swing, or rise,
And challenge even careful eyes.

THE BOWLER

A bowler begins at distant ground,
Then runs towards the crease with sound.
The final stride is quick and strong,
Then suddenly the ball is gone.

Fast bowlers rely upon their pace,
And try to beat the batter's space.
They may use bounce, movement, or speed,
Changing each delivery to succeed.

Spin bowlers work in subtler ways,
Creating puzzles through long days.
Off-spin, leg-spin, flight, and turn
Can make an eager batter learn.

The bowler studies every flaw,
Searching for an opening in the law.
One perfect ball can change the game,
And bring a wicket, fame, and acclaim.

THE BATTER

A batter enters with a plan,
Trying to score as much as he can.
The field is spread, the bowler waits,
And every delivery decides the stakes.

Defense is sometimes just as brave
As a boundary struck beyond the wave.
A patient batter leaves the ball,
Waiting for the right one to call.

Timing matters more than force,
A gentle stroke can change its course.
A powerful hit may look sublime,
But smart batting depends on time.

The batter watches hand and seam,
Predicting where the ball may stream.
Footwork, balance, patience, sight,
All must work together right.

RUNNING BETWEEN THE WICKETS

One run begins with sudden speed,
Two players move with urgent need.
They call for yes, they call for no,
And trust must guide the way they go.

A sharp throw travels through the air,
A fielder waits and watches there.
The wicketkeeper catches clean,
And suddenly the run is seen.

A fraction late, a bat falls short,
And one small error changes sport.
The crease becomes a finish line,
Where centimeters can redefine.

FOURS AND SIXES

A boundary rings across the ground,
As thousands make a thunderous sound.
The ball races along the grass,
And four runs join the batter's class.

A six climbs high into the sky,
The crowd looks up as time goes by.
The ball may land beyond the rope,
And bring a sudden wave of hope.

A cover drive can glide away,
A pull can thunder through the day.
A lofted shot can clear the stand,
And leave a memory close at hand.

THE WICKETKEEPER

Behind the stumps the keeper stays,
Alert through all the changing phases.
Every edge and bouncing ball
Can become a chance to end it all.

The keeper catches, dives, and moves,
Reads the bowler's rhythm and grooves.
A stumping can happen in a blink,
Before the batter has time to think.

The gloves are large, the focus small,
One tiny mistake can lose the ball.
The keeper is a team's final wall,
Ready to react to every call.

FIELDING

Fielding can decide a match,
A diving stop can save a batch.
A boundary saved by fingertips
Can change the pressure on the slips.

A catch may travel high and far,
Beneath the sun or under star.
The fielder runs, then leaps with grace,
And tries to hold it in one place.

A direct hit can end a chase,
A sliding stop can win a race.
Fielding demands courage and speed,
And every player has a need.

TEST CRICKET

Test cricket asks players to stay,
To battle through a longer day.
Five days can hold a thousand turns,
With patience tested as pressure burns.

A batter may spend hours at crease,
Building an innings piece by piece.
A bowler may return again,
Testing the same weakness in rain.

The red ball moves through morning air,
And slips stand ready everywhere.
A session can change the match's shape,
Yet patience gives a team escape.

TEST MATCH SESSIONS

Morning begins with hopes and plans,
While fielders spread across the lands.
The opening spell can tell a tale,
As bowlers search for movement pale.

After lunch the conditions change,
The strategy may rearrange.
The ball grows older, batters settle,
And captains prepare another battle.

By evening shadows cross the field,
Yet neither side is forced to yield.
The final overs can bring surprise,
Under changing evening skies.

ONE DAY CRICKET

One Day cricket brings a faster pace,
With limited overs setting the race.
Batters must score and fielders must chase,
And every over has its place.

The innings moves from start to end,
With wickets lost and runs to spend.
Powerplays create attacking fields,
While smart bowling controls the yields.

A strong total can build the pressure,
But chasing teams may find their measure.
One partnership can change the score,
And open up the winning door.

TWENTY20 CRICKET

Twenty20 cricket moves at speed,
Where every ball becomes a need.
There are only twenty overs to play,
So aggression shapes the day.

Batters attack from early on,
Trying to build momentum strong.
Bowlers vary their pace and line,
Knowing every run can redefine.

Yorkers, slower balls, bouncers too,
Give bowlers many things to do.
Reverse sweeps, ramps, and lofted drives
Show how quickly modern cricket thrives.

POWERPLAY

During a powerplay the field is tight,
And attacking batters see the light.
Only certain fielders can stay outside,
So gaps appear on either side.

A clever batter looks for space,
A bowler tries to control the pace.
The opening overs can set the tone,
And build a total of their own.

CAPTAINCY

A captain reads the match like art,
Knowing when to make a start.
A field is moved, a bowler changed,
And suddenly the game is rearranged.

The captain studies batter and pitch,
Searching for the smallest switch.
One tactical move at the perfect time
Can turn a difficult match sublime.

Leadership is more than calling play,
It means keeping belief alive each day.
When pressure rises and wickets fall,
A calm captain can steady all.

PARTNERSHIPS

Cricket is often won in pairs,
Through patience, trust, and shared affairs.
Two batters build a growing score,
Then find another gear for more.

One may attack while one defends,
Together they can break the trends.
They run between the wickets clean,
And keep the scoreboard moving keen.

A partnership brings confidence,
And forces fielders to defend.
The longer that the pair survives,
The greater pressure grows in sides.

SPIN BOWLING

Spin bowling is a game of mind,
Where flight and turn are intertwined.
The ball can drift before it lands,
Then change direction from its plans.

An off-spinner turns the ball away
From some batters during play.
A leg-spinner uses wrist and flight,
Creating danger left and right.

The googly hides its secret well,
And sends the ball where batters can't tell.
The doosra also tests the sight,
Making spin a battle of the mind.

FAST BOWLING

Fast bowling begins with rhythm,
Speed and balance working within them.
The run-up builds a steady force,
Then the delivery changes course.

A bouncer rises near the head,
A yorker aims near the base instead.
A slower ball can break the pace,
While swing can move through open space.

Great fast bowling is not just speed,
It combines thought with perfect need.
A bowler learns from every ball,
Then returns prepared to challenge all.

SWING BOWLING

Swing bowling uses air and seam,
Making movement like a dream.
The ball may start on one line,
Then curve away by design.

Conventional swing works with shine,
While reverse swing appears with time.
Bowlers use conditions, seam, and pace,
Trying to make batters lose their place.

SEAM BOWLING

The seam can strike the pitch and move,
Creating changes hard to prove.
A ball may leave the bat at peace,
Then suddenly change its release.

On helpful surfaces seamers thrive,
Keeping batting teams barely alive.
They attack the corridor outside,
Waiting for an edge to decide.

THE WICKET

A wicket can mean many things,
A set of stumps with wooden rings.
It can also mean a batter's fall,
A moment that can change it all.

Bowled means the stumps are struck,
Caught means the fielder found good luck.
LBW can end a stand,
When rules place the ball in hand.

Run out happens through pressure and speed,
When batters fail to meet their need.
Stumped means the keeper acts with care,
When a batter leaves the crease unaware.

CAUGHT BEHIND

An edge may make a tiny sound,
But sharp ears hear it all around.
The ball may travel quickly behind,
Where the keeper waits with focused mind.

A slip fielder may take the chance,
Diving low in a sudden dance.
One hand, two hands, the catch is held,
And suddenly the batter is expelled.

SLIP FIELDING

Slip fielders stand close to the bat,
Waiting for edges thin and flat.
The ball can fly in just a blink,
Giving no time at all to think.

They need soft hands and perfect sight,
Reaction speed both quick and right.
A great slip catch may seem routine,
But timing makes it rarely clean.

LBW

Leg before wicket has its own art,
With several rules that play a part.
The ball must meet the legal line,
And other conditions must align.

Umpires watch the ball and pad,
Deciding whether the appeal is valid.
The fielding side may shout with cheer,
While the batter waits to hear.

THE UMPIRE

An umpire watches every play,
Making decisions through the day.
The raised finger can end an innings,
While a wide can give another beginning.

The umpire listens, watches, thinks,
And keeps control when tension links.
Technology may review a call,
But judgment still remains central to all.

DRS

Modern cricket brought review,
Giving teams another view.
If players think a call was wrong,
Technology can help before too long.

Replays show the ball's exact track,
And sometimes bring a decision back.
The process adds another layer,
Where evidence can answer a player.

DUCKS AND CENTURIES

Returns before the day is done.
Cricket calls this score a duck,
A word that hides a little luck.

A hundred runs is a century,
A milestone celebrated proudly.
The crowd may rise and clap with cheer,
As the batter reaches the mark sincere.

Double hundreds demand even more,
With concentration at the core.
Triple centuries are rare and grand,
Remembered across the cricket land.

Six or seven wickets are rarer still,

The bowler may leave the field with pride,

Divide the runs by overs played,

In a chase the required rate

A calm beginning may become tense,
As every boundary gains importance.

A final over can hold the fate,

MENTAL STRENGTH
